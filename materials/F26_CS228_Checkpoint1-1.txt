II. Data engineering (20 points)
Describe:
• Dataset origin: where the data came from (a public repository with its license and access terms,
web collection, or your own collection with the consent of the people recorded), with a citation.
• Dataset characteristics: number of subjects, samples per subject, sessions, image resolution or
signal sampling rate, and the labels (identity, genuine or impostor, live or spoof). Adjust the list to
your data.
• Cleaning and preprocessing: the steps taken for missing values, normalization, alignment or
cropping, and feature reduction (PCA, correlation based selection, and so on), with the reason for
each step.
• Schema sanity and quality: outliers, anomalies, duplicates, or mislabeled samples found at the first
load, and the shortcomings of the data for your problem.
• The split :state the split and data used clearly.

III. Exploratory data analysis (20 points)
Provide some suitable visualization for the data:
• Univariate analysis: histograms and box plots of key features (or of image statistics, signal lengths,
and quality scores when the raw data are images or signals).
• Bivariate analysis: a correlation matrix or scatter plots showing how features relate to each other
and to the labels.
• Class balance: a visual of the class distribution (subjects, genuine against impostor, live against
spoof) and what it implies: oversampling, balanced impostor sampling, or per class metrics.
• Every figure must a title, labeled axes, and one sentence on what you want to express through it.

IV. Baseline implementation and preliminary results (20 points)

repository at the submitted commit.
